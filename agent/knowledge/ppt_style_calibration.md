# PPT Style Calibration

Applies to visual direction, image-page production and visual review. Style
choices are conditional on the brief; no palette or illustrative tradition in
this card is the default for every RCA deck.

## A Working Style Reference

Use current accepted examples across the page roles that matter. Explain what
to preserve: relative color intensity, background temperature, stroke and edge
quality, midtones, transparent layers, material, depth, lettering and spacing.
A named tradition or adjective alone is insufficient. Chinese ink style, for
example, comes from ink modeling, wash, paper and spatial treatment; adding
mountains or bamboo cannot repair unrelated flat vector illustration.

Label each reference by allowed borrowing: content baseline, style, anatomy,
verified evidence/asset or neighboring continuity. Style references contribute
mark-making and material, not sample sizes, labels, claims or organs. A correct
CT/MRI label does not prove correct anatomy or view. Use an explicit anatomical
reference for a medical object when its identity is at risk.

English, Chinese, Arabic/Roman numerals and publication labels all participate
in the lettering style. A paper texture behind rigid printed lettering does
not satisfy a hand-drawn brief. Preserve readable scientific notation and names.

## Calibrate Before Scaling

Choose a small, risk-driven set that tests different roles or loads, such as a
mechanism, result and narrative pivot. Produce and read those pages, compare
with the accepted examples and new neighbors, then continue automatically within
the authorized scope. Already accepted current pages can provide this evidence;
do not add an approval checkpoint or repeatedly regenerate a sample ritual.

Keep one illustrator's palette, lettering, material and space while allowing
different compositions. Consistency does not mean repeated title positions,
card counts, layouts, banners or density. Let evidence and page role determine
complexity; assess both overload and missing explanation. Improve grouping and
composition before discarding necessary proof for whitespace.

## Relative Review And Repair

Review the changed page individually, old versus new, then in its neighborhood.
Check whether it became flatter, rougher, heavier, more crowded or less legible.
Compare actual palette relationships and object modeling, not style-name tags.
Newly accepted stronger pages may raise the benchmark; older pages are not an
aesthetic ceiling. Latest user acceptance determines which benchmark applies.

A redraw can preserve the words and change their meaning through the objects:
exchangeable AI modules and a microscope are not interchangeable metaphors.
Specify unfamiliar object semantics before generation. After a local correction,
inspect the whole affected page for drifting numbers, labels, arrows and anatomy.
Repeated near-misses call for a corrected content/object brief or new composition,
not endless editing of the wrong image. Generalize the failure and correction;
do not turn a user's particular taste into a global style rule.
